# One style family per game

> TL;DR
> - Three style families: cartoony, stylized, realistic. Pick one per game (and per effect) BEFORE choosing textures.
> - Cartoony: chunky simple shapes, saturated colour, snappy squash-and-stretch, may ignore physics; detail weakens it.
> - Stylized: sharp clean abstract shapes, painterly textures, vibrant but not oversaturated, smooth motion with anticipation and follow-through, real timing and size.
> - Realistic: photo or simulated textures, natural colour ramps, slow physics-driven motion; too much detail goes muddy.
> - Mixing families (one photoreal layer in a stylized effect) breaks immersion.
> - Above every guideline: how satisfied the player is with the effect.

## How to choose
Name two "pillars" for the feeling you want (vibrant + friendly, dark + gritty, hand-drawn, sharp + clean) and pick the
family that delivers them. The family decides where textures come from: hand-drawn (cartoony), hand-painted noise and
gradients (stylized), photo/simulation flipbooks (realistic). Source: video 1:22-3:05.

## Traits per family
| | Cartoony | Stylized | Realistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shapes | big, bouncy, chunky | sharp, clear, abstract | real phenomena (dust, debris, embers) |
| Colour | bright, saturated | balanced, not oversaturated | natural ramps (fire orange to blue, smoke light to dark grey) |
| Motion | snappy, exaggerated | smooth, deliberate, anticipation + follow-through | slow, gravity and turbulence |
| Reference | Donkey Kong Bananza, Mario Wonder | Valorant | Battlefield, Call of Duty |

## For vfxmith (ours)
- Make style family an explicit input of every effect brief; reject texture picks from another family.
- Cartoony Roblox simulator games: fewer, bigger layers, saturated, snappy timing.

## Style is the level of shape detail (Ryan Zeng, Shape B 25:01-27:41)
Zeng places the same impact on a line from realistic to stylized: Sekiro (embers, sparks, tiny dust that disperse at
once), Street Fighter 6, Naruto Storm 4 (white spiky ink burst), Zelda TotK (big yellow star burst), Smash Bros
Ultimate (a few bold shapes, a clean ring, a 4-point star). Realistic = more small physical detail; stylized =
simplify and exaggerate the impact moment and add extra elements such as rings or many particles. Use it to place a
brief on the line before picking textures; a cartoony simulator sits near the Zelda/Smash end.
