# Gameplay first and immersion

> TL;DR
> - Effects exist to support the player's actions; tone them down when readability suffers (Jevremovic: gameplay comes first).
> - Single-camera games let you build for that one angle; design to the real camera.
> - A good effect is often one the player barely notices because it obeys the world's rules.
> - Check four things for immersion: a story (what it means), emotion (scared, safe, intimidated), a plausible physics, and transitions (smoke thins out, rain splashes).
> - Roblox translation (ours): ties to the caster-space rule; transitions mean fade ends, ground contact and light reaction.

## Points
- 80.lv: communicate with game design first; effects must be fast but clear; feedback from non-artists; camera knowledge helps. Their categories (environment, character, ability, UI) set how loud each can be: environment effects are subtle unless they lead the eye to a point of interest.
- mad-vfx (Immersion): story, emotions, physics and transition. Physics: even a stylised world needs consistent forces; transitions: fade out and blend elements with depth/soft particles, and let the scene react to a light source.
- UI effects (chest opening, card reveal) build anticipation before the reward; bigger rewards build more.

## Caveat
mad-vfx is generic and offers no numbers; use it as a checklist for review questions, not as a rule.

## Roblox translation (ours)
- Camera: Roblox cameras are player-controlled, unlike League; the rule is [[Build effects in the caster's space]] with a top-down check.
- Light reaction: one PointLight per effect as in [[Value, contrast and glow]].
- Transitions: use ground contact (dust, scorch) and fades from [[Short lingering outros]].
- Reward reveals (rebirth, skins, chests): build anticipation first, then payoff ([[Anticipation, climax and dissipation]]).
